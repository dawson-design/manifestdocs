# Scaling: user-testing notes

Two rounds in September, with eight home cooks each. Round one used a paper prototype of the recipe page with quantities worked out by hand. Round two used an early build of `cookbook show --servings`.

## Who we tested with

- Five cooks who regularly cook for one or two and scale recipes down.
- Six who cook for families of four to six.
- Five who host larger groups a few times a year.

## What we asked them to do

1. Halve Shakshuka for one person.
2. Make Pancakes for three.
3. Scale Potato salad for a picnic of twenty.
4. Scale a recipe of their choice, then cook it at home within the week.

## Findings

### Readers check the header first

Every participant looked at the servings line before the ingredients, to confirm the scaling had worked. Two missed that round one had scaled at all, because the header still showed the original servings. The header must show the servings the reader asked for.

### Large groups are rare and different

Only two participants scaled above 20, and both said they'd use a dedicated catering recipe instead. Above about two dozen, cooking times and pan sizes change too, which scaling can't fix. This is why scaling stops at 24.

### Quantities must be measurable

Round two printed exact results, such as `1.5 eggs` and `22.5 g butter`, and every participant stopped to work out what to do. Several rounded down and the pancakes failed. From round two on, we round scaled quantities: items counted whole round up to the next whole number, teaspoons and tablespoons round to the nearest quarter, and grams and millilitres round to the nearest whole number. Nobody stopped at a quantity after that change.

### Readers want to know the original

Three participants asked what the recipe originally served. We've noted this for the recipe page, but it isn't part of scaling.

## Not pursued

- Scaling by a factor, such as "times 1.5". Every participant thought in people, not factors.
- Converting units when quantities get large, such as grams to kilograms. Participants were happy with large gram values.
